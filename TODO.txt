so basically you're suggesting to remove the mainloop and process that in the irq
Checking whether removing the leftover main-loop call stays safe.

Not the main loop — one of its two calls.

CPU1’s main loop is literally this:
asm

81B4: ANDCC #$EF                          ; enable interrupts
81B6: JSR process_event_b40d              ; drains the $5500 tile-feed ring
81B9: JSR cpu1_run_task_queue_d64a        ; drains the $5320 section-load queue
81BC: BRA $81B6

There’s no game logic in it. All of that lives in the IRQ handlers. The “main loop” is a background worker that drains two queues, so relocating one of the two drains doesn’t restructure anything — it just gives that queue a different owner.

So: move process_event_b40d into the IRQ, called from CPU2’s producer right after INC $B3, draining until $B4 == $B3. Its dependency is within-frame and tight — CPU2 reads the tilemap a few instructions later, in the same $91D4 iteration.

Leave cpu1_run_task_queue_d64a where it is. It has the same structural split (produced by CPU1’s $D2F5-family steppers inside CPU1’s IRQ at $91A7/$91B5, consumed in the main loop), but its timing is far looser — a section crossing happens every 256 px, not every 8 — and it’s the heavy one, loading whole sections. Pulling it into the IRQ would be a real spike for a dependency that currently has frames of slack. If evidence later says it needs the same treatment, it already drains to empty in one pass, so it’s an independent decision.

Once the IRQ is the sole consumer of the feed ring, the main loop’s process_event_b40d call always finds the queue empty. You can leave it as a 4-instruction no-op or delete it — but deleting it is what buys the clean guarantee, because with two callers the IRQ can land mid-feed and you’re back to needing the in_tile_feed guard and the hole it leaves.

Worth checking before you commit: that all four INC $B3 producers really are reachable only from CPU2’s IRQ chain. Your earlier experiment (disabling CPU2’s non-IRQ code didn’t change the scrolling) says yes for the vertical ones, but the two horizontal producers at $8F4D and $9072 are worth confirming, since a producer outside run_frame would leave requests stranded with no consumer.
